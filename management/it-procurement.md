# IT Procurement & Purchase Evaluation

A good IT purchase starts with a defined requirement and ends with a verified handover. Clear specifications and documented evaluation reduce compatibility issues, unplanned costs and delays.

## Procurement workflow

1. **Business requirement:** describe the problem, users or service affected, quantity and required date.
2. **Technical assessment:** confirm compatibility, capacity, security, support lifecycle and dependencies.
3. **Specification:** define mandatory requirements and acceptable alternatives without unnecessarily restricting competition.
4. **Budget check:** estimate purchase, licensing, support, installation, training and recurring costs.
5. **Quotation:** obtain quotations through the organization's approved procurement process.
6. **Evaluation:** compare technical compliance, warranty, delivery, support and total cost.
7. **Approval:** obtain budget-owner and procurement approvals before commitment.
8. **Purchase order:** confirm scope, quantities, price, delivery, payment and acceptance terms.
9. **Receiving:** inspect goods and match them against the PO and delivery documents.
10. **Acceptance:** test the equipment or service against the agreed specification.
11. **Handover:** register assets, licenses, warranty, configuration and support contacts.
12. **Closure:** retain required records and note lessons for future purchases.

## Technical specification checklist

- Intended use and business requirement.
- Quantity, users, locations and expected growth.
- Required performance and capacity.
- Interfaces, protocols and interoperability.
- Power, rack, cooling and physical requirements.
- Security features and management access.
- Software, subscription and license requirements.
- Warranty, replacement and support response.
- End-of-sale and end-of-support dates.
- Delivery, installation, migration and training.
- Acceptance tests and documentation.

## Total cost of ownership

Compare more than the initial purchase price. Include:

- Hardware and accessories.
- Software licenses and subscriptions.
- Support and warranty extensions.
- Installation, configuration and migration.
- Power, rack space and cooling.
- Training and operational effort.
- Consumables and replacement parts.
- Renewal, upgrade and end-of-life costs.

## Goods receipt and acceptance

Verify model, quantity, serials, accessories, license entitlements and physical condition against the purchase order. Run agreed functional tests, record exceptions and obtain sign-off from the responsible service owner.

Keep quotations, purchase orders, pricing, approvals and supplier correspondence in approved internal systems. Do not publish confidential commercial information.
