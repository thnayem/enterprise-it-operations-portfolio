# IT Vendor & Service Management

Vendor management provides a consistent way to evaluate suppliers, monitor service quality, control renewals and resolve issues. The goal is a dependable service and a clear record of commitments, not simply the lowest quotation.

## Vendor record

Maintain an internal record for each supplier:

- Legal and business contact details.
- Products and services supplied.
- Contract owner and internal service owner.
- Contract start/end date and renewal notice period.
- Support hours, escalation route and severity definitions.
- SLA targets, exclusions and service-credit terms.
- Warranty, licensing and entitlement details.
- Data handling, confidentiality and security obligations.
- Open issues, performance reviews and exit/transition requirements.

## Supplier evaluation

Use criteria appropriate to the purchase:

| Evaluation area | Questions |
|---|---|
| Technical fit | Does the solution meet the approved specification and existing standards? |
| Support | Are response, restoration and escalation commitments clear? |
| Reliability | Can the supplier demonstrate references, service history and delivery capability? |
| Security | Are access, data handling, patching and incident notification requirements addressed? |
| Compatibility | Does it integrate with current platforms and licensing? |
| Commercial | What are the total costs, payment terms and renewal conditions? |
| Lifecycle | Are warranty, end-of-sale, end-of-support and replacement plans clear? |
| Risk | Is there dependency on one supplier, proprietary technology or long lead times? |

## SLA review

Track service performance using agreed definitions. A response-time target is not the same as a restoration-time target.

| Measure | Review |
|---|---|
| Availability | Agreed service window and exclusions |
| Response time | Time from valid ticket to supplier acknowledgement |
| Restoration / resolution | Time to restore service or resolve the issue |
| Incident recurrence | Repeat faults and corrective actions |
| Escalation | Whether severity and escalation paths were followed |
| Reporting | Completeness and timeliness of service reports |
| Contract compliance | Deliverables, warranty, licensing and renewal obligations |

## Escalation workflow

1. Log the incident with impact, timestamps and evidence.
2. Open a supplier case using the agreed channel.
3. Assign severity according to the contract.
4. Track acknowledgement, workaround, updates and restoration.
5. Escalate when milestones are missed or impact increases.
6. Confirm service recovery with the internal owner.
7. Request root-cause analysis for significant or recurring incidents.
8. Track preventive actions to closure.

## Quarterly or periodic review agenda

- SLA performance and recurring incidents.
- Open risks, security advisories and lifecycle changes.
- Warranty, license and contract renewals.
- Planned upgrades and maintenance.
- Capacity and forecast demand.
- Outstanding commercial or service issues.
- Improvement actions, owners and due dates.

Keep contracts, pricing, supplier credentials and confidential correspondence in approved internal repositories. Public examples should use fictional supplier names and values.
